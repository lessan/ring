The ring — one page
Reality as of 2026-09-03

Lessan runs a personal AI ecosystem — small repositories on a laptop and a home server, which he calls the ring. In his words it is "part experiment, part frontier pushing, and part useful in the immediate for myself and my family": a way to learn the frontier by putting it under real load, and a way to make regular, marked differences to the household. Publishing is deferred by his own ruling; this page is a bounded exception, shared discreetly.

The shape. At the centre is brain — total memory, a synthesis layer, precedence and relationships, and a governed voice interface. Around it are members, each its own repository with its own vision and its own maturity bar. Researcher takes any topic and makes him an expert on it, producing packages that become new systems. Builder is his executive delegate: it surveys every sibling, finds its own work, carries each to that sibling's bar and stops. Conductor is the rails: one front door for all off-session compute, knowing every subscription's headroom; it cannot refuse work — it answers with a schedule. Domain members (Bahá'í Companion, finance, gardener, home, maker, service, ventures, an autonomous AI game studio) each own a slice of life; dashboard, the home server's record, the jobs adapter and this registry are the estate's infrastructure and outward face.

How work gets done. Nothing important is trusted to one model family. Work is routed across Claude, OpenAI's Codex models, Grok, Gemini and a local model on the home server, by included subscription headroom rather than sticker price. Most work runs as headless jobs in isolated worktrees. Anything gated passes a cross-family review — whoever built it, a different family reviews it against a bar frozen at dispatch, refute-until-clear, and two failed fix cycles stop a lane. Builder runs an hourly tick that moves one lane one step and stops, plus a daily strategic session; anything only the owner can do is queued as a sitting, and the systems are told not to chase him. A supervision contract fixes what the AI may do without asking (its test is reversibility, not confidence) and a doctrine folder distilled from his own words is what autonomous decisions cite. Visions and the contract are his: the AI drafts, he ratifies.

The people and the house. Five people are in scope — the owner, a partner, a daughter, a younger son and an elder son — and this site names them by role only. Personal facts live in a tenant data store that builder is forbidden to touch; that is the one hard boundary. A home server does three jobs at once: household storage and backups, a GPU running local models for anything that must not leave the house, and the ring's own git host; it also serves the household's short links, the daily brief and an ambient e-ink display on the owner's desk.

Status words used below and across the site: live · built and verified · fixture-proven · planned · known-untested. Maturity is each member's position against its own bar; posture says whether it is moving, resting, blocked, dormant by ruling or pre-bar. Weaker word always wins.

How to use this page. Treat it as enough context to give first-level, decision-oriented advice about any part of the ring — a recommendation with its reasoning and trade-offs, not a survey. Lessan decides and the AI advises; say plainly when something falls outside what this page tells you.
