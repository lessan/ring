From a request to finished work

Reality as of 19 August 2026 — Conductor is load-bearing, while its automatic runner is built and verified but not installed.

A conversation may begin the work, but it does not remain the work’s operating environment. The ring turns an intention into a bounded request that can survive the end of the session that produced it.

1. The request names the outcome

Section titled “1. The request names the outcome”

The useful unit is not “run a model.” It is a result wanted by a member.

A request names what should change or be learned, which member owns it, what material may be read or written, and what would count as acceptance. For a build, the bar is frozen when the work is dispatched. That prevents a later reviewer from moving the target after seeing the result.

The request may come from the owner, builder, or another member. Builder usually supplies the semantic layer: why the work matters, how it fits the member’s vision, and where work should stop. The owning member supplies the domain context. Neither of those roles chooses the final execution slot.

2. Conductor is the front door

Section titled “2. Conductor is the front door”

Conductor receives off-session work from any member.

It cannot refuse a request. It returns an ETA class instead: the work can begin now, should begin soon, is waiting for available supply, or is held by a gate. The requester can then keep, revise, or cancel it. Scarcity is not allowed to masquerade as a verdict on whether the request matters.

This division is deliberate. Members bring the what. Conductor owns the when and the with-which-engine. Its record also keeps requests from disappearing when they move out of the conversation that originated them.

The current edge is mechanical. The one-front-door policy is live, and conductor itself is live. Its automatic runner is built and verified, but it is not installed, so wakes remain manual and builder can dispatch directly through the managed machinery. The site does not call that runner live.

3. The engine is chosen by fit and included headroom

Section titled “3. The engine is chosen by fit and included headroom”

The ring uses several model families rather than treating one model as a universal worker.

  • Anthropic’s Claude family is used for orchestration, synthesis, and difficult judgement.
  • OpenAI’s Codex family is used for coherent implementation, hard debugging, and adversarial review.
  • xAI’s Grok family carries substantial build volume and supplies an independent perspective.
  • Google’s Gemini family is available as another off-family reviewer and breadth pass.
  • A local model on the home server is the private and free tier.

The chooser looks first at the shape of the work and the included headroom available across those families. It does not route by a public price list, and this site publishes no plan or allowance details. Scarcer high-judgement supply is preserved for work that needs it; clearer mechanical work goes to the family with room.

The local model has a special boundary. Material that must not leave the house goes there by default, and it can absorb mechanical overflow. It is also the weakest option in the current stack. It is never selected because it is believed to be more capable than the frontier families.

4. The job receives an isolated place to work

Section titled “4. The job receives an isolated place to work”

Fleet work runs in a separate worktree and branch rather than in a human’s live checkout.

That rule was written by a real failure on 9 August: a job changed the branch under a concurrent human session, and an unrelated commit landed on the job’s branch. The work was recovered, but only because the collision was noticed. Isolation therefore protects both sides. The job cannot capture unrelated human work, and the human session cannot unknowingly alter the job’s evidence.

Sensitive jobs receive a sanitised tree before an external engine sees them. Private-lane material stays with the local engine. The execution boundary is part of scheduling, not an optional instruction each requester must remember to add.

5. The result returns as an artefact

Section titled “5. The result returns as an artefact”

The model’s report is not the result by itself.

For code, the result is a bounded change with tests, a branch, and a report that points to the relevant evidence. For research, it is a package with sources, claims, uncertainty, and a handoff to the consuming member. For a review, it is a verdict tied to the bar that was frozen at dispatch.

The requester can inspect the returned artefact without reconstructing the model’s whole transcript. That is the operating preference across the ring: outcomes and evidence are retained; agent chatter is not the unit of trust.

6. A different family gates the claim

Section titled “6. A different family gates the claim”

Work that matters does not merge on its builder’s word.

A reviewer from a different model family checks the returned artefact against the frozen bar. Findings may be accepted or refuted with evidence; the loop continues until the claim is CLEAR or is stopped and batched for a higher-level decision. Only then may the owning workflow merge or activate what its authority permits.

The gate does not prove that the original request was wise, or that a human will value the outcome. It proves a narrower and useful thing: the returned work meets the stated bar at the evidence level claimed.
