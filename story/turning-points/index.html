Eight corrections that changed the system

Reality as of 19 August 2026 — Every episode below is tied to a dated record.

These are not release notes. Each episode changed a rule, a boundary, or the standard of proof used afterwards.

5 August — the board that became a log

Section titled “5 August — the board that became a log”

The runway index had been specified as one row per project. Two days later it held 105 rows and had grown into a long history of individually true updates. The owner-facing board had become the thing one would read instead of the brief.

The rewrite preserved the history elsewhere and restored one current row per project. The rule became: when a tick changes a project’s position, it edits that row. The lesson was not “write less.” It was that appending is cheaper than deciding what the state now is, and a board that only appends is a log.

Source: builder — state/runways/INDEX.md, “Why this file was rewritten on 2026-08-05”.

19 August — the estate was not empty

Section titled “19 August — the estate was not empty”

Three hourly ticks reported an empty estate. The board supported the claim; two siblings’ own tip records did not. They contained seven pieces of already-discovered work that had never been transcribed into runways.

The strategic session re-derived the items at their artefacts. The tick then gained a new rule: before declaring the estate empty, read each relevant sibling’s own tip. It may surface owed work, but it still may not sequence it; strategy belongs to the daily session. The failure was stale aggregation, not idle members.

Source: builder — state/runways/INDEX.md, opening note dated 2026-08-19.

27 July — promotion against the evidence

Section titled “27 July — promotion against the evidence”

Builder brought a trust review with four observations, three of them marks against its own performance, and asked whether authority should be reduced. The owner promoted it instead.

The reason changed the contract’s premise: the scarce resource was the owner’s attention, and an unnecessary gate spent it. Greater authority was paired with stronger undo, logging, and review rather than with a claim that mistakes would stop. Reversibility—not confidence—became the tier test.

Source: builder — supervision-contract.md, preamble and §2.

28 July — consultation stopped being a gate

Section titled “28 July — consultation stopped being a gate”

Builder had drafted that it would not decide consequential things alone. The owner corrected the idea directly: “you DO decide consequential things.”

Consultation remained valuable, especially when something had gone wrong and a fresh perspective could improve the repair. But it became a principle reached for by judgement, not a permission ceremony attached to every difficult decision. That preserved the purpose of the delegation while keeping second opinions available where they earn their cost.

Source: builder — vision.md, “Consultation is a principle, not a permission gate”.

25 July — two gates to be born

Section titled “25 July — two gates to be born”

The researcher’s successor did not become a member because its builder said the work was complete. First, a derived acceptance suite passed under a different model family’s review. Second, the owner ran the acceptance himself.

The two gates answered different questions. The first tested the implementation against a frozen bar. The second established that the person commissioning the system could exercise the result. Only then was the successor pronounced born; the earlier generation became read-only lineage three days later.

Source: researcher-v2 — vision.md, “Lineage — the bootstrap, now history”.

9 August — a live checkout wrote the isolation rule

Section titled “9 August — a live checkout wrote the isolation rule”

A build job was launched in a checkout where a human session was also working. The job changed branches; the human’s next commit silently landed on the job’s branch instead of the main line. It was recoverable only because someone noticed, and no mechanism warned them.

Normal fleet work now runs in isolated worktrees and branches whose boundary belongs to conductor. A direct launch in a live checkout is treated as an exceptional debug or outage risk, not a routing convenience.

Source: skills — jobs/references/routing.md, “Dispatch hygiene — conductor owns execution boundaries”.

19 August — the lookup was obeying the prompt

Section titled “19 August — the lookup was obeying the prompt”

“The web lookup did not hang — it was obeying the prompt.”

A voice interaction appeared to freeze after promising to look something up. The investigation found that the model had been instructed to announce the lookup and stop; nothing in the surrounding machinery guaranteed the promised delivery. The tool path also existed without being called.

The changed rule is broader than voice: a constructed capability that the live composition never invokes is indistinguishable in use from one that was never built. Field behaviour must trace through the actual call path, not stop at the presence of code.

Source: builder — design/ring-site/inventory.md, brain story hooks.

18 August — disclosure became architecture

Section titled “18 August — disclosure became architecture”

The studio’s charter begins by saying what it is. Its fixed disclosure includes “not a person and not a human team,” names human responsibility, and requires every published artefact to say what AI made.

The harder rule sits beside the text: never let a false impression stand; disclose even when doing so costs access, approval, or reach, and record the loss. This turned honesty from a profile sentence into an operating constraint on accounts, contact, publishing, and review.

Source: the studio — CHARTER.md, “My disclosure”.
